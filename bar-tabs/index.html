Project value

Reduce errors and keep service moving during a busy shift.

What the design needed to achieve

  • ✓

    Make every open bill easy to find and understand.

  • ✓

    Protect restricted actions without forcing staff to lose their place.

  • ✓

    Keep table and seat status clear in the list and ordering screens.

Problem

Service changes by the second.

Tabs move between staff, payment states, and service areas during the same shift.

Risk

Slow recovery affects guests.

Unpaid tabs, unclear ownership, and restricted actions create delay and errors.

Direction

Keep state and action together.

The list keeps table status, order status, and the next action together.

Design decisions

Turn service research into visible rules for every tab.

01

Research the pressure

Field understanding

I reviewed ten products, interviewed 15 staff, earned Smart Serve, and worked behind a bar.

Intended impact

The design reflects service speed, errors, training, and low-light conditions.

02

Build one working overview

Visible state

The Bar Tabs Visualizer combines tile and list views, search, quick view, filters, ownership, and status.

Intended impact

Staff can find a bill and understand its current state quickly.

03

Replace drag with selection

Lower learning effort

Testing changed bill splitting from drag and drop to a select-and-assign model.

Intended impact

The split stays explicit and requires less new interaction learning.

04

Keep approval in context

Recover safely

Restricted access originally forced a logout. Manager approval now happens inside the active workflow.

Intended impact

Staff can recover without losing the tab or their place.

05

Cut the first scope

Table Service

I asked for a smaller first release. The team chose a table list, one section, and a separate Table Service mode.

Intended impact

The team could test the basic table flow before adding floor planning.

How the work changed

From broad exploration to a smaller first scope.

Bar Tabs shipped first. The team then narrowed Table Service one decision at a time.

  1. 01 · Research and release

    Build the Bar Tabs base

    I researched service work and led the first flows. A teammate led the shared kit. Product, engineering, and quality teams reviewed the February 2026 release.

  2. 02 · Explore

    Map Table Service

    The first design case covered floor planning, seats, table status, and recovery.

  3. 03 · Reduce

    Start with a list

    I asked for a smaller first release. The team chose a table list and moved floor planning later.

  4. 04 · Refine

    Work through seat actions

    I led ordering while another designer led the visualizer. The design blocks removal of a seat with items. Two options await estimates and user review.
